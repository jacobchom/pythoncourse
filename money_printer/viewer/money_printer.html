Money Printer Prop

A two-roller money maker. Feed blank paper into the front, turn the knob, and a real note rolls out the back. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom.

How the trick works

A strip of black cloth runs between the rollers in an S shape: one end is clipped into the top roller, the other into the bottom roller. Because the rollers and cloth are both black, nobody can see it.

  1. The note starts wound up inside the cloth on the bottom roller.
  2. Put blank paper on the front tray and push it into the gap between the rollers, on top of the cloth.
  3. Turn the top knob towards the back, a bit less than one full turn. The cloth winds onto the top roller and carries the paper with it, rolled up out of sight.
  4. As the cloth leaves the bottom roller, the note underneath is released and slides out onto the back tray.

Loading a note

  1. Turn the top knob forward until most of the cloth is on the top roller. Any paper from the last go comes back out of the front.
  2. Slide the note in from the back tray, under the cloth (between the cloth and the bottom roller).
  3. Turn the bottom knob towards you. The cloth winds back onto the bottom roller and wraps the note up with it. Stop when the note's edge just reaches the gap.

You also need

One strip of thin black cloth or ribbon, about 160 × 550 mm. Lining fabric or a wide satin ribbon works. This is the only part that is not printed; every commercial version of this trick uses one too.

Assembly

  1. Lay one end of the cloth over the slot in the bottom roller and press a cloth clip into the slot to pinch it. Wrap the cloth once round that roller.
  2. Take the cloth up between the rollers and clip the other end into the top roller the same way.
  3. Push the roller axles into one side plate, press the three bars into that plate, then press the second plate on.
  4. Clip the two paper trays into the slots at the front and back, and push the knobs onto the D-shaped shafts.

Print the frame in dark grey and the rollers and knobs in black for the look in your photo. The rollers need a printer at least 200 mm tall.
